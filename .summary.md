## 5. Multi-fidelity discovery: cached parent structure

The decisive empirical observation was that mutation-specific refolding is
often unnecessary for a local single-edit decision even though structural
context itself matters strongly.

Define:

```text
H_j = F(h(mutant_j), G(mutant_j))   # exact-refold/high-fidelity oracle
L_j = F(h(mutant_j), G(parent))     # fixed-parent/cached-context oracle
```

On the complete 2,560-edge ablation:

| Scope | Edge Pearson | Edge Spearman | Sign accuracy | Macro Kendall | Top-5 recall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| train, 2,048 edges | 0.6925 | 0.6771 | 0.7773 | 0.5681 | 0.6906 |
| validation, 512 edges | 0.7441 | 0.7692 | 0.8281 | 0.6042 | 0.7125 |
| all, 2,560 edges | 0.7059 | 0.6967 | 0.7875 | 0.5753 | 0.6950 |

Node-level Pearson/Spearman were approximately `0.9967/0.9964`. This yields the
important general observation:

> Near-perfect value approximation does not imply near-perfect intervention
> approximation, but cached expensive context can still preserve enough local
> ranking to drive a useful high-to-low fidelity cascade.

The 1k-component protocol contains:

- 1,024 unique train SI30 components;
- 128 unique validation SI30 components;
- one anchor and 16 mutations per component;
- 18,432 dense fixed-parent edges;
- exact calibration subset: 1,024 train edges plus all 2,048 validation edges;
- 3,072 exact mutant structures total, 822 already cached and 2,250 newly folded;
- zero fold failures and zero test queries.

## 6. Current headline method: conformal decision gating

The method no longer tries to reconstruct the exact oracle globally. It asks
which candidates must receive expensive exact evaluation to make a controlled
decision.

For one parent/component with candidates `j=1..m`, define exact decision regret
of a queried set `C`:

```text
R(C; x) = max_j H_j - max_{j in C} H_j
```

For epsilon-optimal decisions:

```text
J_epsilon(x) = {j : H_j >= max_k H_k - epsilon}
```

The original absolute-margin nonconformity was:

```text
S(x) = max_j L_j - L_(exact-best)
```

and the candidate set was all mutations within the conformal margin of the
cached-oracle top score. With SI30 component as the exchangeability unit,
split-conformal calibration provides marginal control of excluding an
epsilon-optimal exact decision. It is **not** a deterministic per-protein
certificate.

### 6.2 Quantile implementation detail

The v1 result used a conservative one-order-higher quantile due to NumPy
`method="higher"` indexing. That result is frozen and must not be recomputed.

```python
k = ceil((n + 1) * (1 - alpha))
q = sorted_scores[min(k, n) - 1]
```

## 7. Cost-aware epsilon-optimal formulation

The active optimization problem is:

```text
minimize expected measured high-fidelity compute cost
subject to P(R <= epsilon) >= 1 - alpha
```

The new cost-scaled score uses a frozen predicted runtime `c_hat(x)` and a
normalization reference `c0` learned before fresh calibration:

```text
a_gamma(x) = (c_hat(x) / c0)^(-gamma)

S_(epsilon,gamma)(x) =
    min_{j in J_epsilon(x)} (max_k L_k - L_j) / a_gamma(x)
```

After calibrating `q`, the candidate set contains candidates satisfying the
corresponding scaled margin inequality.

## 10. Confirmatory v2: mandatory execution order

This ordering is part of the scientific protocol and must not be changed:

1. Build exact-sequence PLM features and **cached-parent/fixed-parent** structure

2. Score every confirmatory candidate with the fixed-parent oracle.
3. Apply the frozen fresh-calibration quantiles for both frozen policies.

## 11. Claims that are and are not supported

### Supported

- PLS is a meaningful expensive sequence+predicted-structure oracle.
- Structural/fusion context contributes strongly to local mutation effects.
- Mutation-specific refolding is often less important than evaluating the
  mutant sequence in the parent's structural context.
- Cached-parent scoring preserves useful single-edit ranking at far lower fold
  cost. Over **complete** 19L neighborhoods it is much stronger than the sampled
  16-candidate slate suggested: mean Spearman `0.7111` to the exact field, and
  folding its top 8 recalls the exact best mutation on 7 of 8 pilot anchors at
  `0.0058` of the exhaustive fold cost.
- The reused parent structure, not ESM-2, supplies that ranking power. The
  teacher's own sequence-only ablation reaches `0.1250` recall at the same budget.
- Near-perfect node correlation can coexist with materially lower edit ranking.
- Global residual correction, naive edge KD, and naive discrepancy selection
  do not solve the problem.
- A frozen component-level conformal margin transferred well to 64 new SI30
  components and reduced severe misses versus Top-8.
- Query count materially overstates real GPU savings, and the frozen label-free
  runtime model's predicted savings do transfer: predicted `0.6009` against
  measured `0.6012` on fresh components.
- A correct local gradient of the cached-parent oracle does **not** reach as far
  as one amino-acid substitution. The derivative is verified to correlation
  `0.9797` at `epsilon = 0.05`, and correlation falls to `0.1484` at the real
  substitution scale `epsilon = 1`.

### Not yet supported

- that the v2 gate is more compute-efficient than a fixed Top-M budget on a
  16-candidate slate; the confirmatory run shows it is not, and the tolerant
  policy is dominated by Top-4. This is a statement about that slate, not about
  the cached-parent oracle, which is far stronger over a complete neighborhood;
- any recall or regret number from the neighborhood pilot as an estimate; eight
  anchors give intervals as wide as `[0.47, 1.00]`, and one anchor placed the
  exact best mutation at rank 1016 of 1311;
- conditional coverage for every protein, length bin, or pLDDT bin; the longest
  length bin reached only `0.8438`;
- deterministic certification of the exact best mutation;
- generalization to a second independent scientific oracle;
- superiority over conformal cascades or multi-fidelity best-arm methods, none of
  which have been implemented as baselines;
- that Path-OLD, edge distillation, correction, or discrepancy selection is a
  headline positive method result;
- any gradient-guided, relaxed, or continuously-optimized sequence method on this
  oracle; the one-backward field is indistinguishable from random at useful
  budgets and the failure is a property of the oracle's linear radius, not of the
  implementation.

Use the phrase **component-level marginal risk control** or **marginally valid
conformal decision set**, not "certified best mutation for each protein".

The frozen teacher is **not a pure function of a single sequence**: its
surface-patch pooling makes the logit depend on batch composition, with a tail
reaching `7.18e-02`. Always score through `pls.oracles.score_editflow` with its
configured batch size, and never compare logits produced under different batching.

## 15. One-paragraph project narrative

PLS began as a high-performing sequence-plus-predicted-structure solubility
predictor and is now frozen as an expensive scientific oracle. Attempts to
distill its mutation landscape into sequence-only potentials, direct-delta
models, cycle-consistent fields, or path-aware acquisition exposed a robust
failure mode: good value or global edge fidelity does not guarantee useful
optimization decisions. A stronger empirical structure then emerged: for local
single mutations, recomputing the mutant PLM representation while reusing the
parent's expensive predicted structure preserves mutation ranking surprisingly
well, despite structure itself contributing strongly. Global correction and
absolute-discrepancy selection failed, so the method shifted from predicting the
exact oracle to buying exact refolds only when needed for a decision. A frozen
component-safe conformal margin transferred to new SI30 components, reduced
catastrophic misses, and saved compute, but query count overstated real GPU
savings. The current paper therefore studies cost-aware, epsilon-optimal
conformal gating of cached derived-modality oracles, with a rigorously frozen
fresh-calibration/fresh-confirmatory protocol. Fresh calibration folding, oracle
scoring, and split-conformal quantile calibration are now complete; building
cached-parent confirmatory scores and persisting the gated selection is the
immediate next step, and confirmatory mutant folding stays forbidden until that
selected set is frozen and committed.

### 16.2 The one-backward gradient is dead, and it cost zero folds

Fixing the anchor structure makes the teacher's structure branch exactly
constant, so only ESM-2 needs to be differentiable. `pls.editflow.gradient_field`
does that surgery and reads off `d[i,a] = grad_E_i L . (E_a - E_w_i)`.

| Link | Mean Spearman | Median | Min | Sign agreement |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| one-backward gradient to cached-parent | 0.2284 | 0.2341 | 0.1389 | 0.5739 |
| **cached-parent to exact** | 0.7111 | 0.7261 | 0.4354 | 0.7592 |
| gradient to exact | 0.1699 | 0.1912 | -0.0232 | 0.5761 |
| sequence-only ablation to exact | 0.4420 | 0.4144 | 0.1521 | 0.6604 |
| cached-parent to sequence-only | 0.5551 | 0.6221 | 0.0862 | 0.7043 |

The gradient's top-K is **not better than picking at random** at useful budgets.
It is not an implementation defect: a finite-difference walk along `E_a - E_w`
certifies the derivative and locates exactly where it stops applying.

| epsilon | Median ratio | Correlation | Mean predicted | Mean actual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.01 | 0.8626 | 0.8460 | 0.00028 | 0.00035 |
| 0.05 | 1.0170 | 0.9797 | 0.00140 | 0.00150 |
| 0.10 | 1.0582 | 0.9776 | 0.00280 | 0.00317 |
| 0.20 | 1.1852 | 0.8916 | 0.00560 | 0.00769 |
| 0.50 | 1.1830 | 0.2485 | 0.01401 | 0.09361 |
| 1.00 | 1.2416 | 0.1484 | 0.02801 | 0.17889 |

At `epsilon = 0.05` the prediction is essentially exact. The linear regime ends
around `epsilon = 0.2`, and a real substitution is `epsilon = 1`, roughly five
times outside it. **A correct local derivative simply does not reach as far as a
single amino-acid substitution.**

State the consequence carefully. What is killed is *single-backward gradient
ranking of discrete edits*. Multi-step soft relaxation re-linearizes at every
step and is therefore not the same object as a one-shot extrapolation from
`epsilon = 0` to `epsilon = 1`; nothing here proves it impossible. The correct
verdict is that it is a **dominated research direction**: a forward primitive
that evaluates 10,735 genuine discrete candidates in 143 s already exists, so
paying ESMFold-backward memory costs to obtain a worse continuous approximation
of the same neighborhood buys nothing. Do not spend effort there, and say
"dominated", not "impossible".

### 16.5 The frozen teacher depends on its batch

`pool_surface_patches` derives `label_stride` from the batch-wide maximum
surface-patch component id, so group ordering, the top-64 patch selection and
the `index_add_` accumulation order all move with batch composition.

- reproducing the scorer's batching: max gap
  `1.91e-06` over
  256 nodes, so every frozen score in this repository is reproducible;
- scoring one sequence at a time: median gap
  `7.15e-07` but p99
  `9.76e-03` and max
  `7.18e-02`.

The tail is the same size as a typical mutation effect. No previous result is
invalidated, because all of them used one batching throughout, but the frozen
oracle is **not a pure function of a single sequence** and must always be scored
through `pls.oracles.score_editflow` with its configured batch size.

### 16.6 Canonical scorer contract

Because the teacher's logit depends on batch composition, a score is only
meaningful together with how it was produced. Treat the oracle as

```text
canonical scorer = (checkpoint SHA-256,
                    feature tree SHA-256,
                    canonical manifest node ordering,
                    batch size,
                    batching rule: consecutive nodes, no shuffle)
```

and hold all five fixed within any comparison. In particular the cached-parent,
exact and sequence-only fields of one neighborhood must come from the **same**
manifest order and the same batch size, which is what
`pls.oracles.score_editflow` does when driven from the paired score configs.
Never compare logits across different batchings; differences of `0.01` to `0.05`
can be batch artifacts rather than mutation effects.

Fixing `pool_surface_patches` and training a batch-invariant successor is worth
doing eventually. Do not retrofit it into the frozen v49 story.

### 16.7 Regret, not exact-best identity

Exact-best recall is the wrong headline metric, so the pilot was rescored in
decision units. The result sharpens the bimodal picture rather than softening it:

| Budget | Mean regret | Max | P(R<=0) | P(R<=0.05) | P(R<=0.20) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 0.0875 | 0.6997 | 0.875 | 0.875 | 0.875 |
| 32 | 0.0427 | 0.3417 | 0.875 | 0.875 | 0.875 |
| 256 | 0.0215 | 0.1717 | 0.875 | 0.875 | 1.000 |

Seven of eight anchors have regret **exactly zero** at K=8. There is no
near-miss regime: `P(R <= epsilon)` is flat in `epsilon` from 0 to 0.2. The one
failing anchor still carries `0.1717` regret at K=256, a fifth of its
neighborhood, so a larger budget does not rescue it.

Two label-free findings, both from fields that cost no folds:

- the teacher's sequence-only ablation does **not** contain the missed exact
  best either (rank 509 of 1311 against cached-parent's 1016), but it is
  complementary in regret: `top12_cached + top4_sequence_only` holds the same
  0.875 hit rate at budget 16 while cutting mean regret from `0.0427` to
  `0.0178`, entirely on the failing anchor;
- no tested reliability signal separates the failure. Field-shape statistics
  (top margin, top-K dispersion, MAD, beneficial fraction, effective support)
  and structure-versus-sequence disagreement all land at `|rho| <= 0.58` against
  log rank on eight anchors, and the most-hypothesized signal,
  `struct_seq_top8_disjoint`, points the **wrong way**: more disagreement went
  with better rank. At eight anchors this is noise, but there is currently no
  evidence for the disagreement hypothesis.

### 16.8 Scale campaign in flight

`configs/editflow/pls_neighborhood_scale_v1.json` selects 64 fresh SI30 train

### 16.9 Frozen analysis protocol for the scale campaign

`configs/editflow/pls_neighborhood_scale_analysis_protocol_v1.json` was committed
**before any scale exact fold existed**, and `pls.editflow.scale_analysis` refuses
to run against a protocol that is not committed and clean. This is not
preregistration theatre; it exists because the pilot already showed how easy it
would be to pick a budget, a metric or a baseline after seeing the answer.

Fixed in advance:

- budget grid `K = {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}`;
- primary metric is **exact decision regret** `R_K`, reported as mean, median,
  CVaR95 and maximum, plus `P(R_K <= epsilon)` for
  `epsilon in {0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2}`. Exact-best identity is secondary;
- every policy is charged the **same total exact budget** and compared on the
  loss distribution before any statement about hit rate;
- frozen baselines: cached-parent, sequence-only, the hybrid union
  `Top(B-m) cached + Top(m) sequence-only` for `m in {1, 2, 4, 8}`, the sealed
  gradient control, and a random control;
- failure detection is **exploratory only**, evaluated as ROC AUC and average
  precision for predicting the catastrophic event
  `Y_K = 1[R_K > epsilon]` or `1[rank_cached(best) > K]`, never as a correlation
  against log rank. Any detector that looks good on these 64 components must be
  confirmed on a fresh disjoint batch before it is claimed or used to set a budget;
- the two competing readings of structure-versus-sequence disagreement are named
  in the protocol so neither can be adopted after the fact: `H_unreliable` says
  disagreement marks an unreliable cached field, `H_informative` says disagreement
  marks the parent geometry actually contributing constraint and agreement is what
  signals the structure branch adds nothing;
- mechanism diagnosis on catastrophic anchors (backbone RMSD, TM-score, pLDDT
  change, exposure change, secondary structure change, surface patch change of the
  exact best mutant against its parent) is declared **post-hoc explanatory and
  never a detector feature**, because it needs the mutant's own exact structure and
  so cannot inform a pre-fold budget.

Running the frozen code on the already-unblinded pilot validates it and shows why
the matched-budget rule matters. The hedge is budget-dependent:

| Budget | Policy | Mean regret | CVaR95 | Exact-best recall |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | cached-parent | 0.0875 | 0.6997 | 0.8750 |
| 8 | hybrid `m=4` | 0.1188 | 0.8082 | 0.7500 |
| 16 | cached-parent | 0.0427 | 0.3417 | 0.8750 |
| 16 | hybrid `m=4` | **0.0178** | **0.1421** | 0.8750 |

At `B=8` the hedge is **harmful**: one anchor's exact best sits at cached rank 8,
so surrendering four slots loses it. At `B=16` the hedge is free and cuts mean
regret and tail risk by more than half at identical recall. A hit-rate-only view
would have missed both directions.

### 16.10 Why the cached field fails: the neighborhood, not the optimum

The frozen protocol declares this post-hoc explanatory and never a detector
feature, because it needs the mutant's own exact structure. It costs no new
folds: the exact tree holds each mutant's C-alpha coordinates and the
cached-parent tree holds the parent's, so their Kabsch difference is exactly the
refolding displacement. Statistics below cover **every mutant in every
neighborhood**, 10,735 superpositions, not a sample.

**The literal basin-escape hypothesis is not supported.**

| Anchor | Mutants | Cached rank of best | Best RMSD | Best TM | Best RMSD pct in own nbhd |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 1520 | 1 | 2.645 | 0.6434 | 0.712 |
| **6** | 1311 | **1016** | 2.425 | 0.6296 | 0.455 |

Anchor 7's optimum moved **further** from its parent than anchor 6's and was
still ranked first. Averaged over the two classes the optimum's RMSD percentile
inside its own neighborhood is `0.4546` against `0.4546`, identical. The optimum
leaving the parent's basin is not what breaks the cached field.

What separates them is the neighborhood as a whole:

| Quantity | Catastrophic | Reliable |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `neighborhood_tm_median` | **0.6527** | **0.9471** |
| `neighborhood_tm_p10` | 0.2703 | 0.7475 |
| `neighborhood_fraction_tm_below_0.7` | 0.5881 | 0.1042 |
| `neighborhood_fraction_tm_below_0.5` | 0.2746 | 0.0546 |
| `neighborhood_rmsd_median` | 2.5941 | 0.6729 |

On thresholds: `TM = 0.7` is used here as a **stringent operational marker of
substantial structural drift**, not as a fold criterion. The classical
approximate same-fold transition is nearer `TM = 0.5`, so both fractions are
reported and the earlier phrasing "the median mutant loses the fold" was wrong
and has been removed.

**That correction matters, because at the classical threshold the classes nearly
overlap.** Per anchor:

| Anchor | Cached rank of best | Median TM | Frac TM<0.7 | Frac TM<0.5 | TM p10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **6** | **1016** | 0.6527 | 0.588 | **0.275** | 0.270 |
| 4 | **1** | 0.8742 | 0.316 | **0.237** | **0.240** |
| 7 | **1** | 0.8176 | 0.273 | 0.045 | 0.572 |

Anchor 4 has 23.7 percent of its neighborhood below the classical fold threshold,
almost as much as the catastrophic anchor's 27.5 percent, and a *worse* TM tenth
percentile, and it still ranks the exact best **first**. Only the median and the
`0.7` fraction separate the classes at all, and only by a modest margin over
anchor 4.

So state the support in three layers:

1. **Supported.** The optimum's own displacement is not the cause. A reliable
   anchor's optimum moved further and was still ranked first, and the optimum's
   RMSD percentile inside its own neighborhood is identical across classes.
2. **Supported, descriptively.** Catastrophic failure co-occurred with
   neighborhood-wide structural instability on this pilot: median TM `0.6527`
   against `0.9471`.
3. **Not supported.** That some TM threshold decides whether the cached field is
   reliable. Anchors 4 and 7 are direct counterexamples, and at the classical
   threshold the separation nearly vanishes. Do not assume a rule of the form
   `median TM < t implies failure`. Whether the relationship is threshold-like,
   monotone but noisy, bimodal, or needs a second variable is exactly what 64
   components have to decide, and a co-occurrence on one anchor could still turn
   out to be coincidence.

**Context invalidity is not model disagreement.** These are different failures and
the pilot separates them. A large gap between the cached-parent and sequence-only
rankings means the parent geometry is changing the decision geometry, which is
what the structure branch is for. The dangerous state is instead a cached field
that confidently imposes a structure-conditioned ordering while the true `G(x_e)`
has left that context for most `e`. That is what to look for, and it is why
`struct_seq` disagreement pointing the "wrong" way on eight anchors is not
surprising.

**The probe hypothesis, with its economics stated correctly.** Median refolding
displacement is a *neighborhood* property, so it may be estimable from a handful
of folds. Getting the denominator right matters, because the baseline is no
longer exhaustive refolding but Top-K screening:

- 2 to 4 probe folds are **25 to 50 percent overhead on a `K=8` verification
  budget**;
- they are about **0.3 percent of the exhaustive 19L neighborhood**;
- so the probe pays for itself only if
  `probe overhead < downstream exact folds and regret saved by identifying the
  catastrophic anchors`.

A two-tier policy makes the trade-off concrete. With catastrophic rate `p`, using
`K=8` otherwise and exhaustive refolding on the flagged anchors, the mean fold
fraction is about `(1-p) * 8/19L + p`. At `19L = 3000` and `p = 0.05` that is
about `5.3` percent of exhaustive, roughly **19 times cheaper than folding
everything but roughly 20 times more expensive than plain `K=8`**. On the pilot,
plain `K=8` carries mean regret `0.0875`, all of it from one anchor in eight, so
the question the scale campaign has to answer is whether removing that regret is
worth about 20 times the compute. That is a real decision, not an obvious win.

**If a detector does fire, the fallback probably should not be a larger `K`.** At
cached rank 1016 of 1311, raising `K` from 8 to 256 is buying more tickets in a
broken ordering, which is exactly what the pilot's regret curve shows: `0.6997`
at `K=8` still `0.1717` at `K=256`. The sensible fallback is a different
proposer, or exhaustive refolding for that anchor.

None of this changes the frozen scale analysis. It is post-hoc mechanism, the
probe joins the protocol's exploratory list rather than its frozen baselines, and
nothing here is to be built until the 64 components say whether the catastrophic
regime is stable and how common it is.

**The plot to make when the scale campaign lands**: one point per anchor,
full-neighborhood median TM on the horizontal axis against `R_8` or
`log rank_cached(best)` on the vertical. If the classes separate cleanly there,
the mechanism is settled far better than any black-box detector AUC would settle
it.

One catastrophic anchor out of eight is a lead, not a finding.

## 17. The n=64 result under the frozen protocol

201,399 mutants over 64 complete SI30 neighborhoods, lengths 52 to 296, every one
folded exactly. Zero failures, zero test queries. Analysed by
`pls.editflow.scale_analysis` under the protocol frozen at `1931dbe`, before any
scale exact fold existed.

Compute: 437,373 measured ESMFold GPU-seconds, 121.5 GPU-hours, split across
DiamondHill's seven ROCm GCDs and dd's six RTX 4090s. The frozen runtime cost
model predicted its own hardware's share to within one percent, 226,303 measured
against 228,240 predicted, so it is well calibrated out to length 296; dd simply
ran 1.49x faster.

### 17.1 The cached-parent scan works, and is much weaker than the pilot said

| Budget | Exact-best recall | Mean regret | CVaR95 | Max | P(R<=0.2) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.4062 | 0.4003 | 1.7169 | 2.2047 | 0.547 |
| 8 | **0.5469** | 0.1714 | 0.9498 | 1.3316 | 0.750 |
| 32 | 0.6406 | 0.1236 | 0.8683 | 1.3316 | 0.859 |
| 256 | 0.7656 | 0.0698 | 0.6868 | 1.2336 | 0.906 |

At `B=8`, eight folds out of a median neighborhood of 3,135, so **0.255 percent of
the exhaustive cost, a 392x reduction**, the cached-parent ranking returns the
exact best single mutant on 54.7 percent of components and gives up a mean regret
of `0.1714` against a median achievable gain of `1.683`, about ten percent.

Against everything else at `B=8`: sequence-only recall `0.2031` and mean regret
`1.1188`, the sealed gradient control `0.0156` and `1.6754`, random `0.0016` and
`1.8671`. The cached-parent field carries eleven times less regret than random,
and the reused parent structure, not ESM-2, is what supplies it.

**The pilot overstated it.** Eight anchors gave `0.875` recall at `B=8` with a
95 percent interval of `[0.47, 1.00]`; the truth is `0.5469`, inside that interval
and near its lower end. Reporting the interval rather than the point estimate is
what kept this honest.

### 17.2 The catastrophic regime is not a rare minority

Rank of the exact best under the cached ranking: median 3, p75 159, p90 1,684,
maximum 4,357.

| Event at `B=8` | Rate |
| --- | ---: |
| cached rank > 8 | **45.3%** |
| regret > 0.02 | 43.8% |
| regret > 0.1 | 40.6% |
| regret > 0.2 | **25.0%** |

The pilot's one-in-eight was optimistic. Between a quarter and a half of
components land outside the cheap ranking's reach depending on the threshold, so
the two-tier design that assumed a small identifiable minority does not apply:
at a 25 percent catastrophic rate the fallback dominates the budget.

A larger budget does not rescue them. Thirty-two times the compute, `B=8` to
`B=256`, moves recall from `0.5469` only to `0.7656` and mean regret from
`0.1714` to `0.0698`. The distribution is heavy-tailed, not merely noisy.

### 17.3 The hedge does not replicate; the disagreement signal reverses

**The sequence-only hedge is dead.** At every budget the hybrid union is equal to
or worse than pure cached-parent: at `B=8`, `m=1` gives `0.1733` mean regret
against `0.1714`, and `m=4` gives `0.2014`; at `B=16`, `m=1` ties and `m>=2`
loses. The pilot's single favourable point, mean regret `0.0427` to `0.0178` at
`B=16`, was one anchor's luck and does not survive 64.

**The disagreement signal flips direction and becomes the best detector.** On
eight anchors `struct_seq_top8_disjoint` pointed the wrong way, which was
reported as evidence against `H_unreliable`. On 64 it is the strongest label-free
predictor of failure and points the expected way:

| Signal | Event | ROC AUC | Average precision | Base rate |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `struct_seq_top8_disjoint` | regret > 0.05 | **0.722** | 0.691 | 0.438 |
| `struct_seq_top8_disjoint` | rank > 8 | 0.719 | 0.693 | 0.453 |
| `struct_seq_rank_disagreement` | rank > 8 | 0.710 | 0.616 | 0.453 |
| `top32_dispersion` | rank > 8 | 0.682 (negated) | 0.385 | 0.453 |

So `H_unreliable` is supported and `H_informative` is not: more structure-versus
sequence disagreement means the cached field is less trustworthy, not more
informative. AUC `0.722` is a real but modest signal, and it remains exploratory:
the protocol requires confirmation on a fresh disjoint batch before any detector
is claimed or used to set a budget.

### 17.4 What this leaves standing

- cached-context neighborhood screening is a genuine 392x compute reduction that
  finds the exact optimum about half the time and loses about ten percent of the
  achievable gain on average;
- its failures are common, heavy-tailed, and not fixed by spending more;
- the parent structure, not the language model, supplies the ranking power;
- the one-backward gradient stays sealed as a negative control;
- the only label-free signal with any lift is structure-versus-sequence
  disagreement, at AUC `0.722`, and it needs a fresh confirmatory batch.
